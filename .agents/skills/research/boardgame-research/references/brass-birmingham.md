# Brass: Birmingham — verified rules and turn-order math

Rules below were verified against the official rulebook text (Roxley/Asmodee PDFs
as mirrored by orderofgamers, rulespal, boardgamesbot). Quote these, don't
re-derive them.

## Turn order and actions

- Round 1 turn order is random: "shuffle all players' Character tiles ... in a
  random order on the Turn Order Track."
- Round 1 of the Canal Era: **1 action only**. Every other round: 2 actions.
  The Rail Era starts at 2 actions immediately (no facedown discard, flip the
  old facedown card, shuffle discards, draw 8).
- Money spent goes onto your Character tile; next round the player who spent
  **least** goes first, ties keep their previous relative order, then all spent
  money returns to the bank.
- Income is taken at the end of every round except the final one. Income marker
  starts on space 10; a loan moves it back 3 income LEVELS (not spaces).

## Action costs (round 1 view)

- Loan: **£0 spent**, +£30, income −3 levels, never repaid; banned only if it
  would push income below −10. Legal in the Rail Era right up to the last turn.
- Scout: £0 (discard the action card + 2 more; illegal if you already hold a wild).
- Develop: 1 iron per tile removed, lowest level of that industry only, tile
  goes to the box. Iron from market starts at £1 (both £1 spaces are open at
  setup), then ascending — so the 3rd/4th iron bought already costs £2-£3.
- Network: canal £3, max 1. Rail £5 per link + 1 coal each; a single Network
  action may place 2 rail links for £15 + 1 beer (brewery beer, not merchant).
- Build: tile cost + resources; only Build needs a specific card.

## Resources

- Iron: from any unflipped Iron Works (any player), free, **no connection
  needed**, and 2 irons may come from 2 different works. Market only when no
  unflipped works remain; £6/cube if the market is empty.
- Coal: from the closest connected unflipped Coal Mine (any player), free; if
  equally close you choose one and must take what you can from it before
  moving to the next closest. Market only when not connected to any mine, and
  the purchase needs a connection to a coal-market merchant; £8 if empty.
- Beer: your own breweries need no connection; an opponent's brewery must be
  connected; a merchant barrel only during a Sell at that merchant. Each
  requirement may come from a different source.
- Brewery beer placed on build: **1 barrel in the Canal Era, 2 in the Rail Era,
  for every level** — there is no 3/5/7-by-level scale (a common wrong answer).

## Build, overbuild, sell

- Placement: use a single-industry space when one for that industry exists,
  otherwise a shared space.
- Industry card → the city must be in your network. Location or Wild card → no
  network needed. With **no tiles on the board** (state, not turn number) any
  industry card builds anywhere and any card builds a link anywhere.
- Overbuilding your own tile: any industry, full cost, old resources back to
  supply, old tile out of the game. Opponents' coal/iron only when no cubes
  remain anywhere.
- Sell: needs connection to a merchant showing that industry's icon and that
  tile's beer. Step 5 loops back so ONE Sell action can flip several tiles,
  including different industries, to different merchants. Selling yields no
  cash — it flips the tile and moves income.
- Merchant barrel is consumed with the Sell: Gloucester = free develop, Oxford =
  +2 income, Nottingham/Shrewsbury = VP, Warrington = £5.

## Links and "your network" (the rule people get wrong)

- A placed Link needs **no industry tile next to the line**. It only needs to
  touch a location that is part of your network: "the location contains 1 or
  more of your industry tiles" OR "the location is adjacent to 1 or more of
  your link tiles."
- So your own links extend the network: the rulebook's own diagram says a town
  reached by your link "is a part of your network, even though you do not have
  an Industry tile there." Links therefore chain outward with no buildings.
- Opponents' tiles and links never count toward your-network build checks, but
  they DO count for resource connectivity ("you can trace a route of Link tiles
  (owned by any player)") and for a Sell's merchant connection.
- No-tiles exception: with nothing on the board you may place a Link on any
  undeveloped line — still consuming coal when it is a rail Link.

## End of era / game

- Each era: score links (1 VP per adjacent link icon), then flipped industry
  tiles. Level 2+ tiles survive the Canal Era and can score twice.
- Birmingham scores nothing else: money and income are tie-breaks (income
  first, then cash). The £4→1 VP and income-as-VP rules belong to the
  introductory single-era game.
- £4→1 VP is capped at 15 VP; intro game also scores level 2+ tiles a second time.

## Turn-order simulation (4 players, round 1)

Rank in round 2 = number of players who spent less than you, plus equal
spenders who were already ahead of you in round 1. So £0 actions (loan/scout)
never drop you, and every pound spent can only push you down.

- Seat 1: loan or scout → guaranteed first in round 2 (nobody can spend under
  £0), then claim iron/coal first with the £47.
- Seat 2: loan by default; develop only if the table is develop-heavy.
- Seat 3: loan is strongest here — if both players ahead spent, you jump to first.
- Seat 4: quyết after seeing all three. Anyone spent → loan and jump to first.
  All three loaned → you are last whatever you do, so develop/build costs you
  no position.
- Later developers pay more for iron AND rank worse — the double penalty is why
  developing after someone else is a losing default.
- Community lines worth quoting: "As first player, you can guarantee yourself to
  be the first person to drop an iron in round 2 by either taking a loan or
  scouting as your first action." / "Networking in the first round is very
  dangerous because a player behind you in player order can just take a loan and
  then profit from your link next round."

## Player counts

- Official 2-4. Two players: 10 rounds per era instead of 8, merchant tiles at
  Warrington and Nottingham are removed, market is looser, plans survive longer —
  a good game but a notch below 3-4 (BGG "best: 3-4"). Lancashire is the tight,
  unforgiving one; Birmingham is the flexible #1; Pittsburgh (2027) adds oil
  pipelines, heavy trains and a dedicated 2-player board.
